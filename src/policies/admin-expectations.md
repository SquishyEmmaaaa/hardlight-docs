# Admin Expectations

## Maturity and Discretion

Admins have observer access to sensitive and private in-round information. That access must be handled with full professionalism: no inappropriate sharing, no misuse, and no personal-bias enforcement based on privileged visibility.

## Emotional Self-Regulation

Admins are expected to recognize when they are emotionally compromised or too close to a situation. Passing a case to another admin in those moments is correct conduct, not a failure of duty.

## Unified Front and No Public Staff Disputes

Disagreements with admin actions are handled in private staff channels. Public contradiction of staff decisions undermines trust and is treated as an internal conduct concern.

## Hands-Off, Laissez-Faire Moderation

Admins should not proactively police minor issues without trigger context. Priority intervention is reserved for cheating, server-integrity violations, OOC consent/boundary breaks, and high-impact disruption.

Unnecessary intervention can create a climate of surveillance and paranoia that harms roleplay quality.

## Fun as the Prime Directive

When evaluating edge-case outcomes, the controlling question is whether the ruling supports healthy round quality and broad player enjoyment.

## Proactive but Not Obligated

Admins are volunteers expected to show reasonable initiative and presence, while maintaining sustainable boundaries and avoiding burnout.

## Request Boundaries and Saying No

Admins are empowered to deny player requests when the request is silly, self-serving, disruptive, or would ask staff to bypass normal play or policy for convenience.

This includes requests from players who are trying to avoid conflict, asking to be handed something for ease, seeking a special exception without a strong gameplay reason, or attempting to make staff solve a problem that should be handled through normal play.

If a player is asking for something that can reasonably be obtained through roleplay, they should generally be directed to the in-game, in-character, or roleplay method for getting it. This includes things like items, role name changes, and ship name changes.

| Request Type | Common Response |
|:-------------|:----------------|
| Avoiding conflict or consequences | Decline the request and point the player back to normal play or the relevant rule process |
| Convenience or handout requests | Say no unless there is a clear staff reason to intervene |
| Self-serving exceptions | Decline when the request would create unfair treatment or undermine round integrity |
| Silly, unclear, or low-value asks | Decline politely and, if appropriate, redirect to a better option |
| Request could be earned or handled in RP | Refer the player to the in-game or in-character method instead of granting it directly |

```admonish info
Saying no is not unhelpful when the request is inappropriate. Clear refusal is often the correct and most professional answer.
```

## Ahelp Response Standard

No ahelp should be left unanswered.

Admins should respond to every ahelp with at least an acknowledgment, even if the final answer is no, not now, or another admin will handle it. Acknowledging the player keeps expectations clear and avoids leaving tickets hanging.

| Situation | Minimum Expected Response |
|:----------|:--------------------------|
| Immediate handling is possible | Respond and resolve or continue the interaction |
| Cannot handle right away | Acknowledge the ahelp and state that it is being looked at |
| Request is inappropriate or self-serving | Politely refuse and explain the boundary |
| Another admin should handle it | Acknowledge it and hand it off clearly |
| Ahelp is lengthy or outside in-game authority | Refer the player to a ticket and explain that it needs staff follow-up there |

```admonish info
The expectation is response, not automatic approval. An answered ahelp can still end in a firm no.
```

## Administrative Conduct Expectations

Admins are expected to use communication before punishment where practical, explain expectations and outcomes clearly, apply policy consistently across players, document rationale for significant actions, and focus on behavior improvement over confrontation.

Admins should avoid immediate punitive escalation on non-hardlit matters without a reasonable correction chance (except severe cases), inconsistent standards between players, and escalation when a player demonstrates good-faith correction.
