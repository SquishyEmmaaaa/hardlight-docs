# Admin Structure

This page defines the administration structure at a practical level. Language can be refined later; this version is focused on clear organization and operational planning.

## Role Definitions

### Directors

Directors are the senior leadership layer. They own standards, policy direction, escalation review, and team performance for the areas they lead.

Core responsibilities:

- Set direction and standards for their teams
- Resolve escalated or precedent-setting issues
- Review policy/process gaps and approve improvements
- Mentor Admins and oversee Trial Admin readiness

### Admins

Admins are full staff members responsible for day-to-day enforcement, case handling, and documentation. They execute policy and raise edge cases for leadership review when needed.

Core responsibilities:

- Handle tickets and ahelps
- Apply policy consistently
- Document actions clearly for future review
- Mentor Trial Admins through direct support and feedback
- Serve on one or more teams if they choose

### Trial Admins

Trial Admins are staff in training, operating under supervision while they build consistency in judgment, communication, and tooling.

Core responsibilities:

- Learn policy and escalation flow
- Observe and assist in live case handling
- Produce clear notes/messages/logs
- Escalate uncertain scenarios early

#### Training Pipeline

Trial Admins are trusted with real access on day one. They are expected to respond to ahelps, make calls, and handle routine issues in live conditions.

The pipeline is guidance-based, not rigidly staged:

- Start doing real admin work immediately, with support available.
- Ask for help early when uncertain, especially from Admins or more senior Trial Admins.
- Use each case to improve judgment, systems knowledge, and communication quality.
- Document actions clearly so others can review context and help where needed.

Readiness for promotion is determined through ongoing, practical review by Directors and Admin mentors based on consistency, decision quality, and communication.

## Team Tree

Two directors may lead the same team. Team names below are intentionally broad and long-lived.

### Team Operating Principles

- No team is a command authority over another team.
- Teams own domains, not people.
- Cross-team disagreements are resolved through documented review and director consensus, not unilateral override.

| Team | Team Mission | Owns |
|:-----|:-------------|:-----|
| Community | Keep day-to-day admin response consistent, timely, and player-facing | Ticket/ahelp handling standards, communication quality, handoff discipline, incident documentation quality |
| Mapping & Development | Keep development work organized and deliverable over time | Mapping pipeline, dev backlog triage, implementation coordination, release-readiness checks |
| Balancing | Keep gameplay fair, healthy, and aligned with server goals | Balance reviews, impact analysis, tuning proposals, post-change outcome monitoring |
| Lore & Events | Preserve narrative coherence and support high-quality event play | Lore continuity, event design standards, narrative-facing guidance, content tone alignment |

## Restricted Functions (Not Standalone Teams)

Some duties require elevated trust, credentials, or specialist access and should be treated as restricted functions rather than broad teams.

Examples:

- Infrastructure credentials and host-level operations
- Production deployment permissions
- Sensitive data/database administration
- Policy development and implementation

These functions may be held by a small number of trusted staff while still supporting the broader team structure above.
