## A Note on Playing Security

> *This section is Out-of-Character (OOC) guidance for players choosing Security roles.*

> **From oksam (OOC):**
>
> Security can be one of the most rewarding, engaging, and entertaining roles on the server. However, it comes with it's challanges.
>
> Players who choose to play security roles are empowered with the ability to interrupt player's gameplay. Their authority entrusted in them through their in-character appointment carries an out-of-character impact. 
>
>Often, poor performance by security can lead to frustrations of the playerbase. Security players should apply liberal, generous understanding views when encountering conflict in game. It is not acceptable to "lock them up and throw away the key" - this win first mentality disrupts the community's objectives and creates a negative experience for most involved.
>
>Security players should have a generous understanding of what player antags look like, and how to encoutner them. Being able to distingush the difference between a Head Revolutionary and turned Revolutionary is incredibly important, as it vastly changes the method of interaction with that player.
>
> Security's objective is not to win, just like antagonists, they are expected to contribute to the gameplay loop and provide roleplay.

Some principles to carry into every shift:

- **Talk before you escalate.** An IC conversation almost always reveals more than an immediate arrest.
- **Understand the game mode.** Antags are *supposed* to be doing things — your role is to respond appropriately, not to prevent the round from happening.
- **Losing gracefully is valid RP.** Security doesn't have to win every encounter. Getting outmaneuvered by a clever antag is a good round for everyone, including you.
- **Apply the law, don't weaponize it.** Space Law exists to provide a fair and consistent framework, not as a tool to remove players you find inconvenient.

Severe violations of outlined SOPs may also be treated as an OOC rule issue under [Section 10.1 of Server Rules](../rules/server-rules.md#section-10-command-and-security-rules), since Security and Command are held to a higher standard of role performance.

Security done well is one of the most rewarding roles on the server. You set the tone for the shift.

## RP-First Sentencing

**The purpose of Space Law is to create better roleplay, not to "win" against another player.** Officers, Wardens, and Command are expected to apply the law in ways that preserve scenes, reward cooperation, and let people lose gracefully when doing so does not meaningfully endanger the station.

**Maximum punishment is not the default.** If several lawful outcomes are available, prefer the one that creates the best scene, the clearest consequence, and the most room for continued play. A suspect who is cooperative, entertaining, remorseful, or trying to turn a scene into good roleplay should usually receive more discretion than one who is hostile, evasive, or repeatedly destructive.

Security should actively look for RP-forward alternatives when they fit the facts, including:

- Reducing to a lesser included offense when the circumstances reasonably support it.
- Applying [M13 — Cooperation with Security](crimes-and-punishments.md#m13) generously when a suspect plays along, turns themselves in, or helps move the scene forward.
- Offering work duty, cleanup, restitution, or community service in place of, or in partial substitution for, straight brig time when the offense and station conditions allow.
- Letting a suspect argue for a lower charge or lighter sentence in-character when they make a coherent case.
- Preserving harmless or low-impact gimmick items when keeping them clearly improves the scene and does not create a meaningful security risk.

Examples of good "losing gracefully" and RP-first enforcement:

- If a suspect makes a convoluted but entertaining in-character legal argument, it is acceptable to let that argument partially work when the result is still plausible under Space Law.
- If pirates or similar raiders return and one wants to keep a pet coconut, toy, or other low-stakes gimmick item, security may allow it when the player gives a good in-character reason and the item is not meaningfully dangerous.
- Do not search a PDA, bag, headset, or other item for contraband based on out-of-character suspicion or antag meta knowledge. Searches and escalations must be based on in-character facts, lawful cause, and observable behavior.
- If a prisoner is cooperative and clearly engaging with the scene, offer alternatives to the harshest available outcome: reduced time, work duty, restitution, or a negotiated charge reduction.
- If a crew member is "playing nice," officers should use that to build scene value rather than shutting it down with every possible stacked penalty, search, and enhancement.

This discretion is not permission to ignore serious threats. It is permission to remember that the goal is station roleplay first, legal procedure second, and punitive maximization last.
