# Security Policies & Guidebook

> Operational reference for Security department personnel. This page covers roles, terminology, standard procedures, use of force, and enforcement policy. For evidentiary standards (RS, PC, Preponderance), see [Legal Standards](legal-standards.md). For alert status changes, see [Alert Statuses](alert-statuses.md).

---

## Security Roles

Each Security role carries specific responsibilities and authorities. Personnel are expected to operate within their role's scope and defer to the appropriate chain of command.

### Cadet

The entry-level Security position. Cadets are in training and **must be supervised** by a Security Officer, Warden, or the HoS at all times during active duty.

- May assist with patrols under direct supervision
- May not independently process, sentence, or hold prisoners
- May not access the armory without Warden or HoS authorization
- Should be assigned a supervising officer at the start of each shift
- All actions taken by a Cadet are the shared responsibility of their supervising officer

### Security Officer

The frontline law enforcement role. Officers conduct patrols, respond to reported incidents, make arrests, and investigate crimes in progress.

- May make arrests and conduct searches within authorized parameters
- May access standard-issue equipment from the armory with Warden sign-off
- Must file an incident report for all arrests and notable events
- Reports to the Warden for processing matters and the HoS for operational direction

### Warden

The Warden manages the brig, controls armory access, and is responsible for the lawful processing and timely release of all prisoners. The Warden is the legal guardian of anyone in custody.

- Oversees all prisoner intake, sentencing, timer management, and release
- Controls armory access and equipment distribution to officers
- Should not leave the brig area without HoS authorization except in emergencies
- Responsible for verifying that all sentences are lawful, correctly charged, and properly set
- Must release prisoners when their timer expires unless new charges have been formally filed
- May hold a prisoner beyond their sentence only with documented justification and HoS sign-off

### Detective

The Detective investigates crimes, gathers evidence, and builds cases for prosecution. The Detective operates with more autonomy than Officers but generally does not conduct direct patrols or make arrests.

- May investigate crime scenes and collect physical evidence
- May conduct witness interviews and follow-up questioning
- May request search warrants through the HoS or Captain
- May perform all of the duties of a Security Officer, as this individual is generally a seasoned officer.

### Head of Security (HoS)

The commanding officer of the Security department. The HoS sets operational priorities, authorizes elevated enforcement actions, and is ultimately responsible for the conduct and performance of all Security personnel.

- May authorize arrests, warrants, permabrig placement, and elevated equipment issue
- May declare Blue Alert or recommend Red Alert to the Captain
- May authorize AOS or KOS designations (see Terminology)
- Responsible for the lawful and professional conduct of all Security staff
- Reports to the Captain; must consult the Captain before capital-level actions where possible

> **Chain of Command:** Captain → Head of Security → Warden / Detective → Security Officer → Cadet

## Terminology

Common terms used in Security operations and Space Law.

| Term | Definition |
|:-----|:-----------|
| **AOS** | *Arrest on Sight* — An active authorization to arrest a specific individual upon locating them, without needing to re-establish PC at time of contact. Issued by the HoS or Captain. Must be based on existing PC. |
| **KOS** | *Kill on Sight* — Authorization to use lethal force on a specific individual immediately upon locating them. Reserved for confirmed, active lethal threats where non-lethal options are not viable. Requires both HoS **and** Captain authorization. |
| **Brig** | The Security detention facility. Contains short-term holding cells where prisoners serve timed sentences. Distinct from Permabrig (indefinite holding). |
| **Timer** | The countdown on a brig cell indicating the time remaining in a prisoner's sentence. Must be set accurately — running a timer short or long without authorization is unlawful. |
| **Perma** | Short for *Permanent Confinement* — indefinite brig detention reserved for capital crimes. Requires HoS and Captain authorization. Perma prisoners retain the right to appeal to Colonial Command. |
| **Processing** | The intake procedure for a newly arrested individual: search, charge confirmation, sentence calculation, and timer set. Overseen by the Warden. |
| **Stun** | Non-lethal incapacitation using a stun baton, taser, or equivalent. The preferred method of subduing a resisting suspect in most situations. |
| **C1** | Class 1 Contraband — Minor prohibited items. Typically handled with confiscation and a fine. |
| **C2** | Class 2 Contraband — Restricted equipment that may be legally possessed with a valid C2 Permit. Without a permit, possession is chargeable under [206 — Possession, Contraband Equipment](crimes-and-punishments.md#206). |
| **C3** | Class 3 Contraband — Dangerous or prohibited weapons and materials. Illegal for all non-Security personnel at all times. Possession is chargeable under [306 — Possession, Illegal Inherently Dangerous Equipment](crimes-and-punishments.md#306). Security may carry C3 during Red Alert. |
| **CEDT** | *Contraband Entry Denial Turret* — A statically placed automatic weapon that fires on any individual detected carrying C3 contraband. See [CEDT Policy](#cedt--contraband-entry-denial-turrets) below. |
| **Warrant** | A formal authorization to search a person, vessel, or area. Requires Probable Cause and must be approved by the HoS or Captain. |
| **Alert Status** | The station-wide threat level declared by the Captain or HoS (Green / Blue / Red). See [Alert Statuses](alert-statuses.md). |
| **RS** | *Reasonable Suspicion* — The minimum evidentiary threshold. Allows contact, questioning, and temporary detention. Does not authorize arrest or search on Green Alert. See [Legal Standards](legal-standards.md). |
| **PC** | *Probable Cause* — The threshold for arrest, warrants, and search. Requires articulable facts supporting a belief that a crime has occurred, is occurring, or is about to occur. See [Legal Standards](legal-standards.md). |

## CEDT — Contraband Entry Denial Turrets

Contraband Entry Denial Turrets (CEDTs) are statically placed automatic weapons that fire upon any individual detected carrying Class 3 (C3) contraband. They are among the most powerful deterrent tools available to Security, and their use must be treated accordingly.

### Deployment Policy

- CEDTs may only be deployed at the **explicit authorization of the HoS or Captain**
- Deployment requires a specific, articulable justification — CEDTs may not be placed as a passive precaution or out of convenience
- CEDTs **must not remain active for the entirety of a shift** without renewed justification; deployment should be time-limited and actively reviewed
- When the threat that justified deployment is neutralized, CEDTs must be deactivated promptly
- Failure to follow these guidelines may constitute [220 — Abuse of Authority](crimes-and-punishments.md#220)

### CEDT Firing — Evidentiary Standard

**A CEDT firing on an individual is not, by itself, justification for a search or arrest.**

A CEDT activation establishes [Reasonable Suspicion](legal-standards.md#reasonable-suspicion) for a contact — the responding officer may approach, request identification, and question the individual. The firing is one factor in the [Totality of the Circumstances](legal-standards.md#totality-of-the-circumstances).

Additional observable factors — flight from the officer, continued movement toward a sensitive area, evasive or deceptive behavior, presence during an active alert — may combine with the CEDT indicator to collectively establish [Probable Cause](legal-standards.md#probable-cause) for a search warrant.

**Exception — Blue Alert:** During Blue Alert, a CEDT firing **authorizes a search at the responding officer's discretion**. A warrant is not required when Blue Alert is active and a CEDT has fired on the individual. See [Alert Statuses — Blue Alert](alert-statuses.md#blue-alert).

## Escalation

Violence must be **proportional**. This policy applies to all players — Security, crew, and antagonists alike.

### The Escalation Ladder

Responses must not skip more than one step above the aggression received. Each step up requires a corresponding trigger from the other party.

| Step | Example Actions |
|:----:|:----------------|
| 1 | Verbal argument, insults |
| 2 | Shoving, minor property damage |
| 3 | Unarmed fighting |
| 4 | Armed fighting (non-lethal weapons) |
| 5 | Armed fighting (lethal weapons) |
| 6 | Lethal force / killing |

**Example:** Someone shoves you (step 2). You may respond up to step 3 (unarmed fighting). You may **not** immediately draw a weapon.

### Exceptions

The following situations bypass normal escalation rules:

- **Self-defense against lethal force** — If someone is actively trying to kill you, lethal response is justified.
- **Protecting others** — You may use force proportional to the threat directed at a third party.
- **Security arrests** — Security may use non-lethal force to subdue any resisting suspect. Lethal force is only justified if the suspect poses an active lethal threat.
- **Confirmed antagonists** — Once someone is confirmed as a lethal antagonist (e.g., a confirmed murderer at large), crew and Security may use lethal force to stop them.

### Prior Escalation Carries Over

If someone has already attacked you during the same round, you do not need to restart the ladder from step 1 if they approach you again with clear hostile intent.

### Antagonist Escalation

Antagonists may escalate more freely than regular crew, but **mass violence without narrative purpose is still against the rules**. Having an objective does not grant unlimited license to harm everyone in your path.

> **When in doubt, de-escalate. Ahelp if you believe someone is violating escalation rules.**

## Standard Procedures

### Arrest Procedure

1. **Identify yourself** and state the charges verbally.
2. **Attempt to restrain** — use non-lethal force if the suspect resists.
3. **Search the suspect** — confiscate relevant contraband or weapons per search authorization.
4. **Transport to brig** — do not hold suspects in corridors indefinitely.
5. **Inform the suspect of their charges and sentence** — they have a right to know what they are charged with and how long they are being held.
6. **Set the timer accurately** — the Warden verifies this during processing.
7. **File an incident report** — required for all arrests.
8. **Process and release** when the timer expires, unless new charges have been filed.

### Sentencing Rules

- Sentences must correspond to the charge tier (see [Crimes & Punishments](crimes-and-punishments.md) and the [Sentencing Reference](crimes-and-punishments.md#sentencing-reference) table)
- Multiple charges are served **consecutively** unless the HoS grants concurrent sentencing
- Sentences may be reduced for cooperation or documented mitigating circumstances
- Sentences may be increased for aggravating circumstances or repeat offenses — document the basis for any increase
- Sentences may not be increased arbitrarily or punitively; all adjustments must be justifiable

### Searches

| Type | Authorization Required |
|:-----|:----------------------|
| Pat-down (pockets / hands) | Probable Cause or incident to arrest |
| Full search (bag / ID / equipment) | Arrest, or Probable Cause of contraband |
| Area or locker search | Captain or HoS authorization |
| Search on Blue Alert | Officer's discretion with articulable reason |
| Search after CEDT firing on Blue Alert | Officer's discretion; no warrant required |

All contested searches must be logged. For full search authority by alert status, see [Alert Statuses](alert-statuses.md).

### Permabrig

Permanent Confinement is reserved for capital crimes and requires the following:

- Charges must be at the capital crime level (400s)
- The **Captain and HoS must both authorize** placement
- The prisoner must be formally informed of their charges and that they are permanently confined
- Prisoners retain the right to legal representation and the right to appeal to Colonial Command
- Permanent Confinement does not mean unsupervised — the Warden is responsible for the welfare of perma prisoners

### Use of Force

| Situation | Authorized Force |
|:----------|:----------------|
| Verbal non-compliance | Verbal commands, visible presence |
| Physical resistance | Non-lethal weapons, stun baton |
| Armed resistance (non-lethal) | Non-lethal force + physical restraint |
| Armed resistance (lethal) | Lethal force authorized |
| Active mass casualty threat | All force authorized |

Lethal force should only be used when non-lethal options have failed or are not viable given the threat level. For the full legal framework governing use of force, including the deadly force standard, see [Security Authority](security-authority.md).

> **Reminder:** Security is not judge, jury, and executioner. When in doubt, brig and consult the Captain or HoS.
